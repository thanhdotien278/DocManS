## Product decisions

- **D-001 — Product:** DocManSystem/RTMS is an internal web application for the Military Medical Academy that manages the full lifecycle of university-level research topics and scientific-project workflows.
- **D-002 — Product outcome:** The product is an operational control surface for workflow state, accountability, deadlines, decision bottlenecks, and auditability, rather than only a form or file repository.
- **D-003 — Phase 1 users:** Phase 1 serves system administrators, scientific management staff, leadership/approval authority, principal investigators, project members, and reviewers/committee members.
- **D-004 — Release approach:** The PRD defines phase 1 as a single release for an internal authenticated system using local authentication.

## Scope decisions

- **D-005 — Phase 1 scope:** Phase 1 covers proposal intake and approval, approved-project tracking, seminar and student-research tracking, task management, role-based dashboard/reporting, related-document management, and council/ethics management.
- **D-006 — Supporting scope:** Researcher-profile management, file management, audit logs, in-app notifications, email notifications for important events, and core Excel/PDF exports are part of the MVP.
- **D-007 — Proposal workflow:** The proposal scope includes intake periods, drafts, submission, completeness checks, supplement requests, resubmission, reviewer assignment, scoring/comments, outcome consolidation, approval, and rejection.
- **D-008 — Approved-project workflow:** The approved-project scope includes initiation from approved proposals, milestones, progress reporting, delay visibility, adjustment/extension requests, acceptance, and final review handling.
- **D-009 — Explicit exclusions:** Phase 1 excludes public submission portals, external identity integrations including SSO/LDAP/OIDC/MFA, digital signatures, SMS, deep financial/accounting integration, arbitrary dynamic workflow-engine configuration, and microservices/Kubernetes deployment.

## Permission and governance decisions

- **D-010 — Permission model:** Protected access is determined by system role, organization scope, record participation role, assignment scope, workflow state, and conflict policy; unresolved or ambiguous context fails closed.
- **D-011 — Record-scoped roles:** Principal investigator, project member, scientific secretary, reviewer, council member, and ethics reviewer are record-scoped participation or assignment contexts unless explicitly configured as account-level roles for another purpose.
- **D-012 — Unified workspace:** Users do not switch global roles. Navigation follows the account-level system role, while allowed actions inside each record follow the user's participation on that record.
- **D-013 — Role visibility:** Lists and detail screens state the viewer's role on each record and do not present the account-level role as the record role.
- **D-014 — Administrative separation:** System-administrator authority remains separate from business-decision authority.
- **D-015 — Assignment boundaries:** Reviewers, council members, and committee members can access only assigned items and the supporting context required for those assignments.
- **D-016 — Conflict controls:** Sensitive assignment and decision flows enforce conflict-of-interest and separation-of-duty rules, including blocking self-review, self-approval, and unauthorized scientific-secretary decisions on the same business record.
- **D-017 — Conflict UX:** Actions blocked by conflict policy remain visible but disabled with a plain-language explanation.
- **D-018 — Scope consistency:** Search results, dashboard counts, exports, notifications, history, files, and detail views apply the same authorization and data-scope rules; cross-unit visibility must be explicit and rule-driven.
- **D-019 — Auditability:** Critical authentication, record, workflow, assignment, decision, profile-linking, and important file actions are audit logged with actor, action, timestamp, target record, and sufficient business context.

- **D-020 — System-role cardinality:** Phase 1 assigns exactly one active
  account-level system role to an account. PI, project member, scientific
  secretary, reviewer, council member, and ethics reviewer remain
  record-scoped relationships and do not become stacked global roles.
- **D-021 — Scientific-secretary value and boundary:** Scientific secretary is
  a positive operational role for explicitly authorized administrative data,
  meeting materials, minutes, files, tasks, tracking, and draft summaries on
  the assigned record or council. Secretary status alone never grants scoring,
  reviewer assignment, approval, rejection, or final-decision power.
- **D-022 — Explicit delegation:** Delegated actions require a record-scoped
  grant identifying delegate, grantor, target, permitted actions, validity
  period, and status. Delegation is revocable and audited, and may not widen
  organization scope, bypass workflow state, or override conflict policy.
- **D-023 — Capability response:** Backend authorization is the source of truth
  for record relationships, allowed actions, blocked actions, stable denial
  codes, and plain-language reasons returned to the UI.
- **D-024 — Multiple relationships:** When a user has multiple relationships on
  one record, every security-relevant relationship is preserved and displayed.
  The UI does not infer or display only a “highest role.”
- **D-025 — Acceptance dossier:** When the approved-project workflow requires
  it, the PI prepares and submits the acceptance or final-review dossier before
  authorized staff or leadership makes the final decision.
- **D-026 — Fail-closed delegation assumption:** Wording such as “if
  authorized” in the permission source means an explicit valid grant. In the
  absence of a grant or resolvable context, the action is denied.
- **D-027 — Relationship lifecycle:** Participation, assignment, and council
  membership have status and effective dates; inactive relationships
  immediately stop granting access.
- **D-028 — Co-investigator:** Co-investigator remains a distinct record-role
  label but has no broader default authority than project member unless policy
  explicitly grants a responsibility.
- **D-029 — Internal review disclosure:** Proposal participants cannot see
  reviewer identities, raw scores, comments, or consolidated outcomes before
  the disclosure state; after decision, only a policy-approved summary is
  visible by default.
- **D-030 — Delegation approval matrix:** A current action holder initiates a
  delegable grant and scientific management staff approves it. Draft/file and
  PI submission actions may be delegated; reviewer assignment, scoring,
  membership changes, approval, rejection, and final decisions are
  non-delegable.
